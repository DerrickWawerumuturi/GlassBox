# Editorial vision

## What this is

Glassbox Editorial turns the weekly count of tech job ads into original visual investigations. Each one starts with a question people already ask about the job market and answers it with what the ads say, shown so the reader can check the evidence themselves.

## The promise

**We investigate what technology job ads actually say, and show the evidence so you can decide.**

That is the product's "we show, you decide" applied to writing. An investigation never tells a reader what to learn, apply for or do next. It tells them what the ads say, how we counted, and what the count can't tell them.

## Who it's for

People with a stake in the question: students and new graduates, people switching into software, working developers, the people who teach and advise them, and people who write about hiring. Global in ambition. The count is a selected sample of employer boards that leans toward US and AI companies, and every story says so.

## Personality

Curious, not corporate. Precise, not academic. Direct, warm, sometimes playful. Visually confident, and clear first. Honest about uncertainty without hiding the finding behind caveats.

## Relationship to the product

The product stays practical, fast and trustworthy. The editorial pages may be more expressive: their own composition, chart forms, pacing and interaction. Both share the signals in `VISUAL_LANGUAGE.md`: the wordmark, the fonts, the colour roles, the source and method conventions, the voice.

## What a strong investigation has

1. A question a reader already holds.
2. A finding that survived the checks in `STORYTELLING_GUIDE.md`.
3. A visual idea that belongs to this finding and no other.
4. Writing and composition that let the reader discover the evidence, not just receive it.
5. A phone experience designed on purpose.
6. A reason to share it that has nothing to do with Glassbox.
7. A natural route to the related market page and the CV check, never a pitch.

## What it is not

A content calendar. A place to retell a market page's numbers. A copy of The Pudding. A reason to animate charts or build a framework before a story needs one.
