# Storyboard: Where do the 2 years come from? (concept C)

**Status:** proposed, not approved. Numbers from the 14 Oct snapshot; every one is recounted on publication.

Structure: step by step (short text per step, a phone screen each), not scrollytelling.

## 1. Open
- **Reader's question:** is it true that entry level ads want 2 years?
- **Evidence shown:** 134 unsorted ink squares. Caption: "134 software job ads asking for exactly two years of experience, week of …"
- **Copy:** the title as the question. One line: "We sorted this week's 2,847 software ads by what their titles say. First, a guess."
- **Visual:** the squares fill the first screen beside the title; nothing else.
- **Interaction:** four buttons: "Most say junior or new grad" / "Most say level II or mid" / "Most say senior or lead" / "Most say no level at all".
- **Transition:** the chosen button holds; squares sort.
- **Caveat:** denominator and week in the caption.
- **Phone:** title, squares, four stacked buttons, all above the fold at 390×844.
- **Accessible alternative:** the sorted state and its text summary are in the HTML; buttons are optional.

## 2. The sort
- **Question:** where do they sit?
- **Evidence:** the 134 squares grouped: no level 95 · level II/mid 26 · staff/lead 7 · senior 3 · junior 2 · associate 1. Group labels in Space Grotesk, counts in mono. The reader's guess marked with a small Caveat note ("your guess").
- **Copy:** "Two of the 134 are titled junior. Most have no level word at all: Software Engineer, Backend Engineer, Android Engineer."
- **Interaction:** tap a group for five real titles from it (titles only).
- **Transition:** "So what do the ads that *do* say junior ask for?"

## 3. The junior ads
- **Question:** how many junior titled ads state a number?
- **Evidence:** 72 squares (junior, new grad, graduate; internships excluded). A slider or stepper: "Your guess: __ of 72".
- **Copy:** after the guess: "13." The 13 light lime; 59 stay hatched. "Of the 13, six ask for a year or less, two for two years, five for three or more."
- **Display moment:** the one display sized number on the page: 59 of 72.
- **Accessible alternative:** the answer in text; the slider is a native range input with a label.

## 4. What "no number" says instead
- **Question:** so junior ads expect nothing?
- **Evidence:** a short row of four counts over the 59: mention experience 50 · internships or co-ops 20 · projects or a portfolio 45 · a degree 39 · "no experience required" 0. Marked as a rough keyword read.
- **Copy:** "No number isn't no experience. The ads still talk about experience; they just don't put a figure on it. What a figure would have meant to the person reading, we can't see."

## 5. The complete table
- **Evidence:** the title ladder: six 100% bars, hatch for no number, bands by years, lime for two years. Table twin.
- **Copy:** "Ads with a plain title state a number about half the time, and the number runs from none to eight or more. For those ads the years line is the only level signal there is." Marked as our reading.

## 6. What "2 years" looks like in an ad
- **Evidence:** 88 say "2+ years"; 34 a range starting at 2, one template reading "2–12+ years" on 16 ads; 2 say "up to two years".
- **Copy:** "A range like 2 to 12 is a floor for a whole ladder of roles, not a level."
- **Visual:** three or four quoted fragments set in the reading serif, no employer names.

## 7. Limits
- One week, selected employer boards, about 40% US. The parser reads wording, not intent. Nothing here says how hiring works. Linked method box: snapshot, rules, the internship split, the "up to" fix.

## 8. Explore
- The years page (all software jobs by years), the entry level page, then the CV check, once.
