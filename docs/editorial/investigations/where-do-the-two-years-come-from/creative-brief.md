# Creative brief: Where do the 2 years come from?

**Status:** superseded by the art direction sprint of 14 Oct 2026 (`sprints/2026-10-14-two-years-art-direction.md`). Recommended direction: A, the word that isn't there, with C's structure for the second half. Awaiting the founder's reaction. No build.

## The reader's question
"Every entry level ad wants 2 years. Where does that come from?" The reader arrives with a belief, usually from one or two ads that stung.

## What they should leave understanding
Ads that call themselves junior or new grad mostly don't state a number. The "2 years" sits mostly in ads whose title says no level at all. No number stated isn't no experience expected: those ads still talk about experience, internships and projects. We can't say how anyone screens.

## Central visual idea
**Count the ads.** Each ad is a square, the product's own idiom. The reader says where they expect the 134 two year ads to sit; the squares sort into title groups and the reader sees the 95 plain titles and the 2 junior ones. Then the 72 junior squares; the reader guesses how many carry a number; 13 light.

## Tone
Curious and plain. The reader is never wrong for having the belief; the ads are what they are. No employer is accused of anything. One light touch of wit at most.

## Requirements
- Two guess steps, no more. The answer is shown when JavaScript is off.
- Every number names what it counts and shows its denominator. Hatched grey for "no number", lime for "carries a number" or "asks two years" as the single highlight. Internships outlined where shown.
- Tap a group to see five real titles, so the sorting can be audited.
- The complete ladder as the appendix view, with a table twin.
- A method box: snapshot, rules, the internship split, the location mix, the "up to" fix.
- Explore: the years page, then the CV check, once, at the end.
- Phone composed first: squares reflow, buttons not hover, display number once per act.

## Out of scope
Trends over time. Employer names in the story body. Advice.
