# 2026-10-15: words for a first time reader

Founder, 15 Oct 2026, on the copy rewrite proposal (`docs/local/copy-rewrite.html`):
"I am pretty happy with them, you can change that line above it; if I'm not
pleased we'll just revert". All 56 changes applied (13 must, 32 should, 11 nice),
including the two that needed his yes (P13, H11). Numbers and template variables
are unchanged; no privacy line changed. Decision: brand book section 03, "For a
first time reader", and the decision log row "Words for a first time reader";
`docs/decisions/market-pages.md`, "Words for a first time reader".

## What changed

- **One name for the publication: the weekly count.** "The count", "this week's
  count", "live count" and "the live page" go from visible text. The hub card
  link says "Latest count: {page} jobs"; the stories say "the weekly page".
  /product's receipt is "The weekly count", with a note that joins it to the
  scan above it ("Your dashboard counts the jobs that fit your CV. The weekly
  count on Market counts all of them."). The home "How it works" lead names it.
- **"Read" explained where the reader meets it.** A market page's dek:
  "In the week of 12 October 2026 we found 561 entry level software jobs at 196
  employers. 559 had an ad long enough to read for skills, and 276 of those name
  Python." Every later "we could read" is "we read". The method fold says one
  sentence on all ten pages (`readLine` in `lib/market-story.ts`): "We look for
  about 250 named skills, by fixed rules, in the 559 ads long enough to read. The
  other 2 were too short."
- **The week, said less often.** Byline "Counted by Glassbox · Week of 12 October
  2026 · a new count every Monday" (one "Counted"). Squares caption, FAQ answers
  and the rail drop it; the FAQ says "Answers from the week of …" once under
  "Questions this page answers", and the FAQPage markup adds "Week of …" to each
  answer so a search snippet stays dated (`seo.ts`). Story bylines drop
  "counted in"; the story week line after the CV ask is one short line.
- **Captions that lean on what was said.** The languages caption defines
  "senior" and gives the breadth rule's reason; the contrast caption says "Same
  rule as the languages" (the full rule when the page has no languages figure).
  Three different figure subtitles where there was one. "Mention" and "list"
  beside "name".
- **The CV ask.** Market pages and the years page put a question beside the
  button: "Curious how many of the 15 skills these jobs ask for most are already
  on your CV?" (`ASK.lead`, `MarketCvAsk lead`). The sheet: "How many of these 15
  are on your CV?" and "We read the skills on your CV and compare them with the
  15 that 559 entry level software jobs mention most." Stories and the hub ask
  "Curious how many of these skills are already on your CV?". The phone menu's
  "Try it out" is "Compare your CV".
- **Hub:** H1 "The week in tech jobs"; a dek that says what the two kinds of
  card are; story pills "Story", their filter "Stories".
- **Home:** subtitle "Each week we count which skills thousands of tech jobs ask
  for, and how many of them you already have."; "counted this week" gone from
  the count sentences and the wall; the scan line says it runs on the latest
  jobs, not the Monday count; a new FAQ, "Why once a week?".
- **/product, /method, /about:** product dek "One PDF in. Four pages out, each
  one about where you stand."; chapter 1 "The skills your jobs ask for"; the
  Opportunities caption without "stretch" and "tier"; /method's back links;
  /about's receipt link and "we read" (its equivalents of R3 and P3, the page
  was rebuilt after the proposal).

4. /about step 4: "The weekly count goes up every Monday."
5. /about receipt: "… backend jobs we read." and its link "The week in tech jobs" (the
   about page's own copies of P3 and R3).
6. Market pages' rail: the nav's aria label "Also this week", matching its heading.
7. Market and years pages' sheet result and after scan line: "skills … jobs
   mention most" (the ask's `what`, so P15 reads as proposed).
8. A story's sheet line: "… compare them with the 11 this story counts in 559
   entry level software jobs." A story picks its skills, so "the ones its jobs
   mention most" would not be true there.
9. Peak Python's method fold: "The weekly count is published every Monday."
10. The lobby's floor note: "titles the weekly page lists" (S4's intent).
11. Contents may vary's box footnote: "The weekly page gives no count for this line" (S4).
12. A market page's structured data fallback headline: "What {page} jobs ask for" (P1).

Not changed: the stories' own prose, covers and share images still say "we
could read" in places (open in the brand book, section 14).
